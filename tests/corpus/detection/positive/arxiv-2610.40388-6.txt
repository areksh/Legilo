For ideal data with negligible errors for all six quanti-
ties of r and v, the above relations provide the equations
that uniquely determine all six parameters and no statis-
tical analysis is necessary. For real data with uncertain-
ties, the only issue is how to implement the six relations.
Here, it is important to note that if any of the six quan-
tities in the left side of Equations (3) and (4) is(are)
essentially exact (i.e. extremely precise with negligible
error(s)), the relation(s) including that quantity(-ies)
provide(s) the mathematical constraint(s) (i.e., consis-
tency relation(s)) for the involved parameters, and the
quantity(-ies) need(s) not be included in a likelihood of
the fitting problem. This means that the mathematical
constraint(s) reduce the number(s) of free parameters
because one parameter per constraint is automatically
determined from the other parameters.
