The same condition also appears in the first-passage
problem of quantum systems monitored by repeated mea-
surements at a fixed time interval T. If the starting site
of a quantum walk is measured once per interval un-
til the walker is first found there, the mean number of
measurements is an integer [10, 11], as first observed on
a quantum computer for a single qubit [12]. This in-
teger drops abruptly at values of T for which Eq. (1)
holds for two eigenstates that overlap the starting site
[13]. Restarting the walk broadens these drops by an
amount set by a time-energy uncertainty relation [14].
Related degeneracies produce fractionally quantized re-
currence times in interacting spin systems, which has also
been demonstrated experimentally on a superconducting
processor [15]. We show that the entropies of the reset
stationary state have resonances at the same condition.
As the exchange coupling strength or the transverse field
of the Hamiltonian is varied at fixed step duration, both
entropy diagnostics show narrow peaks and dips along
the loci where Eq. (1) holds (Sec. IV D). An entropy, a
quantity not usually associated with periodic dynamics,
therefore marks where two eigenvalues of the step unitary
coincide, as the mean return time does.
In classical stochastic processes, resetting is known to
speed up search [3, 4]. For example, the mean first-
passage time of a diffusing particle to a target, which
diverges for free diffusion, becomes finite under reset-
ting to the initial position and passes through a mini-
mum as the resetting rate is varied [3]. This observation
has been developed into a general theory of first pas-
sage under restart [4, 16]. Restarting also reduces the
mean detection time in monitored quantum walks [17],
and related restart effects are found in a quantum walk
with flat-band localization [18]. In a monitored quantum
