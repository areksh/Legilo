However, despite its prosperity, there also exist findings reporting deficiencies of
solely relying on online annotated reading. Paper-based annotated reading demonstrated merits over its online counterpart for it gives readers the opportunity to slow
down and add their own thoughts and comments, raise questions for further investigation, and challenge ideas within the text [17] while relieving the burden of hardware
requirement [18]. Furthermore, both Aydogdu and Winder [19], and Chen and Chen

group dynamics, participation, motivation, and deeper learning compared to online
modes.
To make full use of the pedagogical benefits of paper-based annotated reading, offline group interaction and social reading, we designed a blended four-stage
annotated reading instructional framework. A quasi-experiment with mixed research
methods was conducted in an EFL context to verify its impact on students’ reading
competence as well as explore students’ perceptions of and actualization of their
annotated reading practices. The findings would have implications on annotated
reading instruction and it is also our hope that this instructional design will help inform
future studies.

2. Literature review
2.1. Annotated reading and pedagogical frameworks
Annotated reading, also known as close reading, is a deliberate interaction with a text
with the aim of helping the reader to understand it more fully [17]. Sustained attention can be conceptualized as the core cognitive underpinning for annotated reading.
Öksüz et al. [21] demonstrate that sustained attention serves as a “cognitive bridge”
during reading—it not only directly predicts comprehension levels but also acts as
a full mediator, transforming the efficacy of verbal working memory into accessible reading comprehension ability. Thus, the practice of annotated reading—which
requires readers to continuously select, mark, and reflect on text segments—can be
seen as a natural behavioral expression of sustained attention, training the very process of consistently and strategically allocating attention to key information.
