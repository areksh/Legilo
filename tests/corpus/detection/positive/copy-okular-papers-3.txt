Abstract
Annotated reading is generally believed an effective way to improve learners’ reading
competence. However, most existing studies focus on online annotated reading while
neglecting to scaffold students throughout the entire learning process. To address the
gap, this study proposed a four-stage annotated reading framework and employed a
quasi-experimental design to evaluate its effectiveness in developing students’ read-
ing competence. Concurrently, students’ perceptions and actualization of this peda-
gogy were also investigated. Results demonstrated that this framework had potential
in cultivating students’ reading competence, it was perceived as offering substantially
more benefits than difficulties, and students who yielded greater gains exhibited
higher engagement levels. Furthermore, modes of learning were not the decisive
factor in learning outcomes, and students’ engagement proved equally critical. The
findings can facilitate the field’s re-examination of technology use and shifting more
focus to students’ individual differences and the holistic teaching-learning process.
Targeted pedagogical suggestions were presented based on the findings.
1. Introduction
Reading competence, the ability to read and understand what has been read, is con-
sidered a key competence that remains important throughout a person’s life [1], and
ranked among the most pivotal language skills in language education [2]. In English
learning, reading can not only expand learners’ vocabulary [3,4] but also improve
their language output abilities [5], and the transfer of reading skills can be applied to
other language skills such as listening, speaking and writing [6]. Therefore, develop-
ing students’ English reading competence is one of the prominent responsibilities for
English teachers.
