A.1.3

W ORD ALIGNMENT AND EPOCH CONSTRUCTION .

We reconstructed transcript words by matching the dataset’s transcript entries to the corresponding
sentence text, recovering punctuation and contractions where applicable. When multiple separately
timed entries mapped to a single sentence word, we retained the separately timed entries. Each
resulting word received a persistent transcript index linking its embedding to its neural epoch.
Movie timestamps were mapped separately to each subject’s neural recording using the supplied
synchronization triggers. Words lacking valid onset or offset times, or beginning after the final
synchronization trigger, were excluded from neural alignment.
For each remaining word, we rounded its recording-relative onset to the nearest sample on the 512Hz preprocessing grid and extracted a high-gamma epoch covering [-2,2) seconds relative to onset.
When multiple words mapped to the same onset sample, we retained the first; epochs extending
beyond the recording boundaries were discarded. Each extracted epoch was then independently
resampled to 32 Hz using FFT resampling with a Hamming window, yielding 128 samples per
contact at 31.25-ms intervals. Consequently, every epoch used a sampling grid anchored to its own
word onset.
We stored the retained transcript indices alongside the epochs and used these indices to select the
corresponding word embeddings. Analyses combining subjects used the intersection of their retained word indices. The iEEG analyses additionally restricted words to those covered by the 300dimensional GloVe vocabulary, ensuring a common vocabulary across model comparisons.
