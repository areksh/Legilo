Annual Report of the Riverside Natural History Society
For the year ending 31 March 2026
Foreword
This report records a year of steady work by the Society's members and volunteers. The collections have
grown, the reading room has opened on two further evenings each week, and the long-planned survey of the
river meadows has at last begun. We are grateful to everyone who gave their time, and to the trustees who
kept the finances sound through a difficult winter.
The pages that follow set out the year's activities, the state of the collections, and the accounts. As in previ-
ous years, we include an extended essay drawn from the Society's lecture series, chosen by the committee as
the most widely discussed.

The Year's Lecture
With respect to the horse, I have collected cases in England of the spinal stripe in horses of the most distinct
breeds, and of all colours; transverse bars on the legs are not rare in duns, mouse-duns, and in one instance in
a chestnut: a faint shoulder-stripe may sometimes be seen in duns, and I have seen a trace in a bay horse. My
son made a careful examination and sketch for me of a dun Belgian cart-horse with a double stripe on each
shoulder and with leg-stripes; and a man, whom I can implicitly trust, has examined for me a small dun
Welch pony with three short parallel stripes on each shoulder.
