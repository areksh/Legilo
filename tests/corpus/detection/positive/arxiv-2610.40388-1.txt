S. M. Saad & Y.-S. Ting (2026) have recently questioned the K.-H. Chae (2026) algorithm for using a
mathematical relation of parameters including a fixed
s to reduce the number of free parameters. They argue
that treating a as a free parameter along with an empirical prior rather than letting it be determined by the
mathematical relation can dramatically change gravity
inference. Their claim is unexpected because the mathematics of Keplerian orbits is fixed and the tiny measurement error of s ensures that it can be used as a fixed
input into the mathematics, as pointed out above.
Considering the unexpected claim by S. M. Saad
& Y.-S. Ting (2026), here I consider a purposely extended algorithm in which the semi-major axis is explicitly treated as a free parameter and the measured
sky-projected separation s (along with its measurement
uncertainty) is included in a likelihood (but not used

Chae
as a constant in a mathematical relation), and present
Bayesian results for example binary systems including
the α-Cen - Proxima system and systems from the K.H. Chae et al. (2026).
In this work, I carefully address the question of what
the proper modeling of wide binary orbits should be in
the posed problem of inferring gravity at low acceleration and testing gravity models using wide binaries. It
turns out that the introduction of a free parameter for
the semi-major axis has negligible effects in the Bayesian
inference of orbit parameters and Γ compared with the
implicit-a algorithm of K.-H. Chae (2026). I will then
show that the S. M. Saad & Y.-S. Ting (2026) claim of a
Newtonian result from the K.-H. Chae et al. (2026) sample may be a consequence of arbitrarily enlarged errors
for the measured quantities, in particular the relative
radial velocity between the two stars. In this work, I
will also explicitly show that the parameters of Proxima’s orbit inferred with the K.-H. Chae (2026) algorithm fully agrees with the P. Kervella et al. (2017) inference although two studies use completely independent
algorithms and work in different reference frames. Then,
I will present orbits obtained for MOND (M. Milgrom
1983, 2010) gravities.
The outline is as follows. I start with a brief description of the extended algorithm that includes the semimajor axis as a free parameter, highlighting only the
difference from the K.-H. Chae (2026) algorithm (Section 2). In Section 3, I present results with the extended
algorithm and compare them with other results including those with the K.-H. Chae (2026) algorithm and
Proxima’s orbit inferred by P. Kervella et al. (2017).
I discuss and conclude in Section 4. In Appendix A, I
discuss how the perspective effects of the spherical geometry can be accurately accounted for through coordinate transformations. The extended algorithm treating
the semi-major axis as a free parameter will be available
on Zenodo.
