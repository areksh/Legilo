requiring no model in the loop, and the gradient never reaches the response.
Shallow alignment. A complementary line of work locates the mechanism of these failures: align-
ment behavior concentrates in the first few output tokens (Qi et al., 2024a), which is why pre-
filled affirmative openers (Andriushchenko et al., 2025; Vega et al., 2023), decoding-parameter ex-
ploits (Huang et al., 2024), and fine-tuning perturbations of early positions (Qi et al., 2024b) defeat
refusal. Proposed remedies deepen the alignment, e.g., by augmenting training with recovery-from-
harmful-prefix data (Qi et al., 2024a; Zhang et al., 2025a).
6 CONCLUSION
We present Assess → Decide → Respond, a multi-turn defense in which the defender commits to an
explicit, parseable safety decision before it replies, trained by reinforcement learning at the payload
turn. Rather than repairing shallow alignment, the framework repurposes it: the short span that
decides the fate of a response, ordinarily the attacker’s target, becomes a commitment the defender
controls. Because the decision is a token span, a rule can reward it against the conversation’s known
nature with no judge and no annotation, and the same format also supports a judge-based reward with
safe redirection. Across two model families and fourteen benchmarks, our defenders achieve the
strongest multi-turn safety in a controlled comparison, hold that lead under six judges, and preserve
utility, with over-refusal as the cost. KL analysis, interventional probes, and ablations agree that the
defense operates through the committed decision. More broadly, our results suggest that structural
properties of alignment, often treated as weaknesses, can be engineered into defenses, and that
commitments offer a scalable alternative to judge-based supervision for conversational safety.
