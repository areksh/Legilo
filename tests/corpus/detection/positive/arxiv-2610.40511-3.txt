BACKGROUND AND M OTIVATION

2.1

L ANGUAGE E NCODING M ODELS

Encoding models test whether a candidate representational space captures information present in
neural responses. In language neuroscience, this approach has progressed from investigator-defined
semantic features to contextual representations learned by neural language models, which predict activity across fMRI, MEG, and intracranial recordings (Huth et al., 2016; Jain & Huth, 2018; Toneva
& Wehbe, 2019; Schrimpf et al., 2021; Goldstein et al., 2022; Caucheteux & King, 2022).
LLM representations are effective encoding spaces because they capture many aspects of language
simultaneously, but this also complicates their interpretation. A single layer may contain thousands
of distributed features reflecting lexical, syntactic, semantic, contextual, and predictive information,
any combination of which may support neural prediction. Thus, successful encoding establishes
that neurally relevant information is linearly accessible from the representation but does not reveal
which components carry that signal.
